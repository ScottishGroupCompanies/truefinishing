Glenwood Springs sits where the Roaring Fork meets the Colorado River, with I-70 running through it. West Glenwood and downtown see the same winter film of salt and magnesium chloride that the highway carries, and it ends up in entries, garages, and small commercial kitchens.

That film is removed by grinding, not by a cleaner alone. Entries and garages are an [epoxy](/floor-finishings/epoxy-flooring/) conversation after [preparation](/floor-preparation/). Dining rooms and shops that can leave the concrete exposed are [polished concrete](/floor-finishings/concrete-polishing/). Basements that take hillside water need a [moisture test](/floor-restoration/concrete-moisture-mitigation/) before a covering.
