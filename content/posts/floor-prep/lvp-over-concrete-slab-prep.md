**You can install luxury vinyl plank over concrete when the slab is flat, clean and within the manufacturer moisture limits. Most LVP products call for flatness within about 3/16 inch over 10 feet, a moisture test such as ASTM F2170, and repair of cracks and high spots. Skipping prep causes gaps, peaking, clicking joints and adhesive failures.**

## Can You Put LVP Directly on Concrete?

Yes. Luxury vinyl plank is one of the most popular finishes for basements, condos and commercial tenant spaces built on concrete. It is waterproof at the surface, comfortable underfoot and relatively quick to install. But “waterproof” refers to the plank, not the system. The slab underneath still has to meet the manufacturer’s requirements for flatness, moisture and cleanliness.

Most installation problems with LVP over concrete trace back to the slab: dips that let click joints flex, humps that make planks rock, or moisture that feeds mold under the floor and breaks down adhesive.

## Flatness: The Most Overlooked Requirement

Floating click-lock LVP is very sensitive to flatness. Many manufacturers require the floor to be flat within 3/16 inch over 10 feet or 1/8 inch over 6 feet. Always check the specific product’s installation guide because requirements vary.

### How to check flatness

1.  Lay a 10-foot straightedge in several directions across the room.
2.  Measure gaps under the straightedge with a tape or feeler gauge.
3.  Mark high spots and low spots with a marker.

### Fixing high and low spots

-   **High spots and ridges** are ground down with a diamond grinder.
-   **Low spots** are filled with a patching compound or a cement-based self-leveling underlayment.
-   **Whole-room unevenness** may call for a full self-leveling pour. See our [concrete leveling](/floor-restoration/concrete-leveling/) page.

## Moisture Testing Before Vinyl

Concrete holds water long after it looks dry, and basement slabs can take on vapor from the soil. Vinyl is a vapor-tight surface, so moisture that used to evaporate from bare concrete gets trapped beneath it.

The two common tests are the calcium chloride test (ASTM F1869) and the in-situ relative humidity probe test, [\>ASTM F2170](https://www.astm.org/f2170-19a.html). Many flooring manufacturers now reference RH testing, with limits often listed between 75% and 90% RH depending on the product and adhesive. The installation instructions decide which number applies.

### When moisture is too high

Options include a moisture-mitigating epoxy primer, an approved underlayment with a vapor retarder, or waiting for the slab to dry. Our [moisture mitigation](/floor-restoration/concrete-moisture-mitigation/) service installs systems rated for high-RH slabs.

## Cleaning and Removing Old Flooring Materials

Old carpet tack strips, tile mortar, paint, cutback adhesive and sealers all interfere with a good LVP installation, especially glue-down planks. Grinding removes these residues and gives patching compounds and adhesives a sound surface.

-   Pull tack strips and fill the nail holes.
-   Grind off thinset ridges and old adhesive.
-   Test unknown black adhesives for asbestos before disturbing them, especially in older buildings.
-   Vacuum thoroughly before priming or leveling.

## Floating vs. Glue-Down LVP on Concrete

| Installation | Pros | Prep priorities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floating click-lock | Fast, DIY-friendly, easy repairs | Flatness and moisture barrier/underlay |
| Glue-down | Quiet, stable, good for commercial and rolling loads | Clean, porous surface, moisture within adhesive limits |

Commercial spaces with rolling chairs and carts often use glue-down LVP, which makes surface cleanliness and moisture testing even more important.

## A Simple Prep Checklist for Installers and GCs

1.  Confirm product flatness and moisture requirements from the installation guide.
2.  Test moisture and document the results.
3.  Remove old flooring residue by grinding.
4.  Repair cracks and expansion joints as the manufacturer recommends.
5.  Grind high spots, fill low spots, or pour self-leveler.
6.  Vacuum and verify flatness again before installation.

## Common LVP-Over-Concrete Mistakes to Avoid

Most callbacks on vinyl plank installed over concrete come from a short list of avoidable mistakes. Knowing them helps homeowners, installers and general contractors plan the schedule and budget honestly.

### Skipping the straightedge

A slab can look flat and still have a dip near a floor drain or a ridge along an old pour joint. Click-lock planks bridge those areas and flex every time someone walks across them, which eventually breaks the locking edges.

### Assuming a basement slab is dry

An old basement slab may look dusty dry on the surface while still carrying moisture vapor from below, particularly after a wet spring. Testing takes a day or two and is far cheaper than replacing a moldy floor.

### Using the wrong patch

Not every patching compound is rated for use under resilient flooring, and some need a primer to bond. Using the manufacturer-approved patch and primer protects the flooring warranty.

### Leaving no expansion space

Floating floors need perimeter gaps as specified by the manufacturer. Concrete does not move much, but the vinyl does with temperature, and Colorado sunrooms and walkout basements can swing widely through the day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a moisture barrier for LVP on concrete?

Often yes, especially on basement or slab-on-grade floors. Many floating LVP products require a 6-mil polyethylene sheet or an underlayment with a built-in vapor retarder over concrete. Glue-down products rely on adhesive moisture limits instead. Always follow the specific manufacturer instructions, and test the slab before installing.

### How flat does concrete need to be for vinyl plank?

Many manufacturers require flatness within about 3/16 inch over 10 feet or 1/8 inch over 6 feet, though this varies by product. Floors outside that tolerance can cause click joints to flex, separate or squeak. Grinding high spots and filling low areas with patch or self-leveler brings the slab into tolerance.

### Can LVP go over cracked concrete?

Yes, if the cracks are stable and flush. Small shrinkage cracks are filled and ground smooth before installation. Cracks with height differences need grinding or patching. Actively moving cracks should be evaluated first because movement can telegraph through glue-down vinyl or stress click joints over time.

### What happens if you install LVP on a damp slab?

Trapped moisture can cause mold or musty odors beneath the floor, adhesive breakdown in glue-down systems, and voided manufacturer warranties. Moisture may also cause planks to shift or curl at the edges. Testing with ASTM F2170 or F1869 before installation is the best way to avoid these problems.
