## What Surface Preparation Involves

Concrete surface prep involves a series of steps designed to create the right texture and cleanliness level for optimal coating adhesion. Here’s what that typically includes:

-   **Assessment and testing:** Checking moisture levels and concrete condition.
-   **Cleaning:** Removing oils, dirt, and existing coatings using industrial-grade cleaners and equipment.
-   **Mechanical grinding:** Using diamond grinding or shot blasting to create a roughened surface profile (Concrete Surface Profile, or CSP).
-   **Crack repair and joint filling:** Sealing existing cracks to ensure smoothness and uniformity in the final finish.

Following these steps helps create a surface with the right bond profile, ensuring that your new floor coating performs as intended under all conditions.

Further, research by the [National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)](https://www.nist.gov/topics/construction) shows that following standardized surface prep protocols can extend the service life of concrete coatings by up to 40%. That means fewer repairs, less downtime, and better returns on your investment — especially crucial for commercial and industrial facilities.

## Common Coating Issues Caused by Poor Prep

Cutting corners at the prep stage may save time upfront but often leads to costly issues down the line. Here are some of the most frequent failures due to poor surface preparation:

-   **Delamination:** Coatings peel away from the concrete due to poor adhesion.
-   **Bubbling/Blistering:** Trapped moisture leads to raised bumps under the surface.
-   **Uneven texture:** Skipping grinding steps results in inconsistent finishes and poor aesthetics.
-   **Premature wear:** Coatings break down sooner due to weak bonding and environmental stresses.
