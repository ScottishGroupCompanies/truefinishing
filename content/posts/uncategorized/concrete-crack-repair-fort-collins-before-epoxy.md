## Why Repairing Concrete Cracks Before Epoxy Matters

Concrete may look tough, but it’s not immune to cracking. These cracks can form due to temperature fluctuations, heavy traffic, moisture infiltration, or settling over time. Applying epoxy directly over damaged concrete without repair can dramatically reduce the effectiveness and longevity of the surface. In fact, a [study published in the International Journal of Adhesion and Adhesives](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0143749613000774) found that improper surface preparation prior to epoxy repair can cut the bond strength in half. That’s a 50% reduction in durability caused solely by skipping an essential first step.

Proper crack repair is not only about performance but also aesthetics. Epoxy coatings are designed to create a smooth, seamless appearance. Cracks that are not adequately filled and restored beforehand can “telegraph” through the coating, resulting in unsightly lines and uneven texture that compromise the high-end look homeowners and businesses seek.

## Best Practices in Concrete Crack Repair

Concrete crack repair is about more than just filling gaps; it’s a strategic process aimed at restoring structural integrity and preparing for optimal adhesion. Here are the key steps we follow at True Finishes:

### 1\. Inspection and Assessment

We begin with a comprehensive inspection to determine the type, width, and depth of the crack. Differentiating between structural and surface-level cracks is critical since each demands a different approach. For instance, static cracks may only need filling, while active or spreading cracks might require flexible sealants or reinforcement.

### 2\. Cleaning the Surface

Cracked areas are cleaned thoroughly to remove debris, dirt, and loose particles. This is crucial to ensure any repair material bonds directly to solid concrete rather than contaminants.

### 3\. Crack Filling

Depending on the crack profile and the intended epoxy application, we’ll use either a rigid repair material like epoxy injection or a more flexible polyurethane filler. Each material is selected based on its compatibility with the planned floor system to ensure chemical bonding and structural cohesion.

### 4\. Surface Preparation

Even after crack filling, surface preparation isn’t over. We mechanically grind the entire substrate to create the right texture and porosity for epoxy adhesion. According to [research from the American Concrete Institute](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=269), repairing cracks before epoxy application can extend the life of the repair by approximately 40%—a compelling reason to get this step right.
