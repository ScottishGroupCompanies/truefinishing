## Why Choose Epoxy Flooring?

Epoxy flooring is known for being extremely durable, waterproof, chemical-resistant, and visually appealing. It’s become a go-to option for both residential and commercial spaces across [Colorado communities](/cities-we-serve/). Applied as a resin that hardens into a seamless coating, epoxy creates a solid, resilient surface that can last for years with proper installation and maintenance.

## Can Epoxy Be Applied Over Vinyl Flooring?

The short answer is: Yes, it is technically possible to apply epoxy over vinyl flooring. However, it comes with critical caveats and requires specialized expertise and preparedness to ensure a successful and long-lasting finish.

### Risks of Applying Epoxy Directly Over Vinyl

While it might be tempting to apply epoxy directly over your existing vinyl floor in order to save on time and demolition costs, this route can be risky. If the vinyl beneath is not properly prepped—or, worse, if it’s loose or compromised—it can lead to adhesion problems, bubbling, or outright peeling of the epoxy coating.

In fact, a [study by the American Coatings Association](https://www.paint.org/) shows that effective surface preparation can increase epoxy adhesion by as much as 75% when applied over vinyl substrates. Skipping or shortcutting the preparation process may save time initially, but it will almost certainly shorten the lifespan of your epoxy flooring.

### What the Research Tells Us

Scientific data supports what we at True Finishes have long known from hands-on experience. A [2018 study published in the Journal of Coatings Technology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/15421406) found that epoxy installations over unprepared vinyl saw failure rates up to 50% higher than those treated with proper substrate preparation techniques. Such numbers highlight just how critical professional prep work is to flooring longevity and performance.

Further reinforcing this, [ASTM International guidelines](https://www.astm.org/) indicate that more than 80% of premature epoxy floor failures stem from insufficient surface preparation—many of which occur over existing vinyl or similar substrates. When it comes to commitment and craftsmanship, prep work simply isn’t optional—it’s essential.

## What Does Proper Floor Preparation Look Like?

At [True Finishes](/), we follow a systematic and proven method to prepare vinyl floors for epoxy installation. This includes:

-   Thorough cleaning to remove dirt, oils, and residues
-   De-glossing the vinyl surface to create texture and promote adhesion
-   Conducting adhesion tests
-   Filling seams, cracks, or gaps for a smooth substrate
-   Grinding or abrading the vinyl if needed to ensure proper mechanical bonding

Learn more about our process at our dedicated [floor preparation service page](/floor-preparation/).

### Should You Remove Vinyl Before Applying Epoxy?

While it is possible to apply epoxy over vinyl in situ, in some cases the most reliable and durable route may be to remove the vinyl entirely. This is especially true for older vinyl tiles that may harbor asbestos (common in homes built before 1980), or if the vinyl is noticeably warped, lifting, or loosely adhered to the subfloor.

If your vinyl has these issues, don’t worry. Our trained surface preparation specialists at True Finishes will conduct an on-site evaluation and determine whether epoxy over vinyl is a viable option—always factoring in your specific space, budget, and timeline.

## Why Denver Homeowners Must Pay Extra Attention

[Denver’s unique climate](/cities-we-serve/floor-finishing-concrete-polishing-denver-co/), with its wide seasonal temperature swings and potential for moisture intrusion, makes proper flooring preparation even more critical. Vinyl flooring tends to expand and contract with temperature changes, and if this movement transfers to your epoxy layer, it can compromise structural integrity over time.

This is why Denver residents should always consult local experts who thoroughly understand how to install epoxy coatings that will last in Colorado’s challenging conditions. Trusting a nationally recommended method without adapting it for local climate can be a costly mistake.
