Concrete microtoppings are a thin layer of concrete-based material applied over existing surfaces to refresh, smooth, or enhance the appearance of concrete or other substrates. They’re often used for decorative purposes and can transform both indoor and outdoor spaces.

### Key Features of Concrete Micro Toppings

-   **Thickness:** Typically 1/16 to 1/8 inch thick, making them great for minimal elevation changes.
-   **Versatility:** They can be applied to various surfaces like old concrete, wood, or tile, and can mimic the look of polished concrete, stone, or even a smooth, sleek finish.
-   **Customization:** Available in a wide range of colors, textures, and patterns. You can also add dyes, stains, or other decorative elements to achieve custom designs.
-   **Durability:** While thin, microtoppings are strong and resistant to wear and tear. They’re ideal for high-traffic areas.
-   **Applications:** They’re commonly used for floors, walls, countertops, and even outdoor patios or driveways. The finish can be smooth, textured, or patterned, depending on the aesthetic you’re aiming for.
-   **Easy Maintenance:** They provide a surface that is easy to clean and maintain, especially when sealed properly.

### Benefits of Concrete Micro Toppings

-   **Restoration:** Micro toppings are a great solution for revitalizing old or damaged concrete without the need for full replacement.
-   **Cost-effective:** They offer a more affordable option compared to tearing out and replacing surfaces.
-   **Fast Application:** The process is relatively quick, often completed within days, making it a great choice for both residential and commercial projects.

If you’re considering concrete micro toppings for a flooring project, they provide an excellent way to achieve a modern, clean aesthetic while improving the durability and functionality of the space

## What a microtopping is

Microtopping, sometimes called microcement, is a thin cementitious coat troweled over a prepared floor. The photos on this page show wood-look color and seamless interiors. It is closer to an [overlay](/floor-restoration/concrete-overlay/) than to [polished concrete](/floor-finishings/concrete-polishing/). Polish grinds the slab you already have. A microtopping covers it.

Thickness is the reason people choose it: it can go over a sound floor without building the height of a new structural slab. It still needs a clean, sound, and usually dry substrate. [Floor preparation](/floor-preparation/) and a [moisture check](/floor-restoration/concrete-moisture-mitigation/) come first. A floor that is out of flat may need [leveling](/floor-restoration/concrete-leveling/) before a thin finish, because a skim coat will follow the dips.

## Where it works

Interiors that want a continuous cement look, including rooms where the existing concrete is patched, stained, or already covered with a topping that cannot be polished cleanly. It is a design finish. A forklift aisle or a garage full of tire salt is usually an [epoxy](/floor-finishings/epoxy-flooring/) problem instead.

## Pros and cons

You get color and a seamless surface the old slab did not have, at a low added thickness. The finish is only as stable as the floor under it. Cracks in the substrate can telegraph. It is not a way to fix a heaving slab.
