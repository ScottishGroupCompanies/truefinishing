Prep floors and concrete the right way. Make sure your concrete overlay or polish, tile, carpet, or any other finishing, will be made to truly last.

## Substrate & true Floor Subfloor Preparation

Success in flooring installation relies on a strong bond between the substrate and the underlayment or topping.

At True Finishing, we ensure the surface is fully prepared keep a strong bond. Regardless of the topping or underlayment used for leveling, smoothing, or repairing the substrate, its strength relies on the quality of the surface it adheres to. The surface must be clean, sound, and free from substances like oil, grease, wax, dirt, asphalt, curing compounds, latex, gypsum, dust, paint, or any other material that could hinder bonding. We prioritize and are known in Colorado for our over-preparing the true floor subfloor to guarantee a successful bond.

## Concrete Floor Prep FAQ’s

Common questions asked when ready to prepare your floors.

How much dust will floor preparation create?

The amount of dust created during floor preparation varies depending on the method used. For example:

-   **Shotblasting** generally produces minimal dust because the system often includes a dust collection unit.
-   **Grinding** can generate a significant amount of fine dust, especially with dry grinding, unless dust extraction systems are in place.
-   **Scarifying** and scabbling create more debris and coarse dust, as they aggressively remove surface material.
-   Using vacuum systems or dust extraction units can minimize the dust generated during these processes.

Dust levels can also depend on the size of the area and the condition of the existing floor and what needs to be done. Contact us to get a quote and ask about your specific details.

## Why prep decides the finish

Every finish page on this site points back here. Epoxy is diamond-ground or shot-blasted. Polish is a grind. Tile and vinyl need ridges and adhesive gone. The epoxy page states that dust left after grinding is a cause of peeling, and that the company runs grinders, shot blasters, and dust collection.

## Process

1. Identify the existing floor: coating, adhesive, tile, carpet, or bare concrete.
2. Choose the mechanical method. Grinding and shot blasting are the ones named on the service pages.
3. Remove the dust.
4. Confirm flatness and moisture before the finish. Those are [leveling](/floor-restoration/concrete-leveling/) and [moisture mitigation](/floor-restoration/concrete-moisture-mitigation/) when the tests say so.

## Pros and cons

Mechanical prep is loud and dusty even with collection equipment, and it is why a floor can be coated the right way. Skipping it is cheaper on bid day and is the usual reason a floor fails. There is no chemical shortcut on these pages that replaces grinding for a professional coating.
